# Make workplace actions explain themselves visually

**Rebuild the handbook as one compact clipboard with interchangeable illustrated work slips, and make its diagrams match the objects players actually handle.** Business as Usual currently asks players to interpret unfamiliar stations, floating shapes, and instructions that assume knowledge they have not acquired. Changing fonts cannot resolve that mismatch. The recommended direction combines warm printed paperwork, expressive Bricolage Grotesque headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible reading text, and a single current action supported by a recognizable physical target. A hood opens on a vehicle; a battery sits in its compartment; a package rests on a dispatch counter. Solo play must finish the same conserved work loop through explicitly identified unattended services. These are design recommendations for the rescue implementation, not claims that the redesign or gameplay improvements already exist.

Research date: **12 October 2026**. **Verified** means supported by linked primary descriptions or documentation. **Observed** means a visual reading of the particular published image or user screenshot, not every screen in a game. **Proposed** means a BAU adaptation or design judgment. No reference game was installed for this research, and no human usability session was conducted. Reference screenshots are linked to their publishers; their availability does not grant permission to reuse their artwork. This report supplements the earlier competitive research while retaining **Ordinary jobs, extraordinary suspicion**.

Design handoff: the [Figma design workspace](https://www.figma.com/design/J2QJX5ZjjCNuMgbK2achnI) was created after this research. Its existence does not establish completed mockups, rendered comparisons, or Unity implementation.

**Animal Crossing: New Horizons.** Nintendo describes gathering materials, consulting recipes, and crafting, with distinct collections held in the NookPhone. **Papers, Please.** Verified developer material centers inspection, searching, and fingerprinting around specific documents. Receipts, service tickets, and shipping labels need different hierarchies. Multiplayer work cannot require laborious rearrangement merely to read one action.

**Unpacking.** Verified descriptions explain pulling possessions from boxes, fitting them into rooms, and understanding a life through objects. The developer states that completion does not require reading and offers control and animation accommodations. ([Developer site and accessibility statement](https://www.unpackinggame.com/)) The inspected [bedroom screenshot](https://unpackinggame.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Screenshot02.png) provides distinct object silhouettes and visible receiving surfaces. First-person framing needs closer target cues than Unpacking's room overview. Familiarity is not universal; an electrical probe still needs an illustration.

**Good Job!** Nintendo verifies multiple solutions to workplace puzzles, including wheeling a projector through a doorway or propelling it through a wall, with time and damage affecting replay scoring. **LOCKDOWN Protocol.** The publisher verifies real-time first-person communication and cooperation to complete tasks while hidden dissidents obstruct progress. ([Publisher description and screenshot gallery](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/)) Proposed BAU lesson: useful spatial work creates reasons to meet, wait, assist, and observe. Teach the repair through an exposed component, a nearby tool, and a visible outcome. BAU's criminal must maintain a believable working life rather than simply stop all work.

**Dale & Dawson Stationery Supplies.** Specialists perform objectives, Slackers imitate productivity, and a Manager observes and fires employees. The publisher describes proximity discussion and ordinary coffee breaks among productive employees. ([Publisher description and screenshot gallery](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2920570/Dale___Dawson_Stationery_Supplies/)) Proposed BAU lesson: normal work should support competing explanations. A worker inspecting a returned package can have a legitimate reason without the interface declaring that reason true. Adapt the workplace ambiguity; reject managerial ejection as the basic loop. Exact task animation and interface organization remain unverified here.

The shared inference is **object → action → observable result**. None of these sources proves a BAU mechanic will be fun. The practical test is whether a new player recognizes the target, performs the action, and understands the result without external explanation.

## The current screens explain systems instead of work

Observed in the user's screenshots, the handbook gives oversized text most of the left page while leaving the right page nearly empty. Binder decorations cross the reading region; tabs float above a composition that offers little useful comparison. Its instruction assumes the player knows what probes are and why an incoming battery needs checking. The gameplay HUD repeats profession, progress, a large NEXT paragraph, map guidance, and an interaction prompt. A prompt can say **Place parcel** while rejection says **Not available yet**. That combination tells the player neither whether placement succeeded nor what corrective action to take.

Observed world defects are equally instructional: cream slabs appear unsupported, small display vehicles hover above a workbench, and a hood-like panel opens without convincingly identifying the engine compartment. Large props, fist poses, and labels compete with the actual target. These observations describe the supplied views; they do not establish every collider, desk dimension, or animation defect in the build. Shrinking a desk without checking object bounds and the standing camera could simply create a different scale mismatch.

**Proposed information contract:** normal play exposes profession, current task, small progress, compact map, and a contextual prompt. Carrying adds one modest object thumbnail/count. Remove the permanent explanatory NEXT card. JOB shows the immediate action with a large diagram and a small sequence; ITEMS explains the physical load; RECORDS contains permitted documents; TOWN opens the expanded map. Detailed reasons live behind an optional disclosure. Inventory names should say **Replacement battery**, with a short identity field available on inspection, instead of giant floating registry identifiers.

**Proposed mechanic introduction:** hood illustration and **Open Hood** lead to the actual latch. The hood rotates around a real hinge, revealing the battery. A probe illustration and **Check Battery** point to its posts; an instrument reading supplies the result. Replacement, verification, closing, and signing become relevant in order. Optional thorough checks must communicate their consequence and progress, rather than require an unexplained second press. HUD objectives target 3–6 words, prompts 2–4 words, and quick hints one short sentence, ideally under 12 words. These are the user's design targets, not measured comprehension thresholds.

**Proposed solo entry:** the menu's primary action is **Start Solo**, opening one window and selecting a profession. Unattended services use the same item identities and custody transitions. An arriving delivery is labeled **Town Courier**, with a physical package and a service-attributed receipt. Never claim another human performed it. Each profession must complete its own shift without waiting for absent colleagues; Host and Client remain explicit secondary choices.

## Printed workplace graphics support one compact clipboard

Verified MUJI principles emphasize materials, simplified processes, and simplified packaging; its packaging team describes common packaging bodies differentiated by stickers and product information. ([MUJI principles](https://www.muji.com/jp/about/en/), [Packaging-team interview](https://careers.muji.com/jp/story/package-material/)) An observed [Meiji package image](https://www.meiji.co.jp/products/brand/simplepackage/images/pkg_mv.png) uses cream packages, strong product names, object illustrations, narrow color bands, and aligned quantities. Proposed BAU lesson: build a common label grid and vary the product image and role accent. Japanese retail influence should provide hierarchy and economy, not untranslated ornamental text.

Verified TWI instruction material separates important steps, key points, and reasons, and progresses through preparation, demonstration, attempted work, and follow-up. JISHA situational illustrations place hazards within transport, cleaning, and machine work. ([TWI examples](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/training-within-industry-twi/), [NIST overview](https://www.nist.gov/mep/training-within-industry-twi), [JISHA illustration overview](https://members.jisha.or.jp/login/intro/kyt/kyt_index.html)) Public pictograms are a distinct standardized information system. ([JSA overview](https://webdesk.jsa.or.jp/common/W10K0010/?page_id=JIS_Z_8210_1&post_type=book_common)) Proposed BAU lesson: an original instructional card contains one verb, one object, a motion arrow, and a recognizable result. Draw a hood/body hinge, battery/probes, or bag/chute; a generic wrench cannot distinguish all mechanic actions. These sources do not authorize copying their illustrations.

Verified GS1 guidance distinguishes readable text/graphics, transport details, and identification codes, connecting a physical parcel to its records. ([Logistic-label guideline](https://ref.gs1.org/guidelines/logistic-label/)) Proposed BAU documents use that hierarchy without claiming commercial-label compliance. A shipping label emphasizes destination, object, and quantity; a receipt aligns what/quantity/when; a service ticket records vehicle, observed reading, work, and signature; an inventory tag records identity and count. **Not recorded** differs from **Failed**. No document becomes an omniscient custody history or guilt verdict.

The following are **proposed layout concepts**, compared with identical repair content.

| Concept | Gameplay advantage | Main risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clipboard with interchangeable slips | One focal surface; diagram, quantities, and signature belong together | A large clip or stacked papers can hide controls | Recommended; keep clip outside the content frame |
| Small ring-bound field notebook | Portable personality and dated records | Turns delay comparison; rings invite obstruction | Reserve its character for record styling |
| Folded instruction sheet | Strong short action sequence and route overview | Folding hides steps; wide diagrams struggle during resizing | Optional induction/route card |
| Two-page manual | Useful for comparing two real records | Empty second page and excessive width repeat current faults | Only an explicit comparison view, if needed |

**Recommended composition:** a small profession/title strip, a dominant current-action illustration, one verb/input line, a five-step thumbnail strip, and one relevant count or reading. JOB / ITEMS / RECORDS / TOWN attach to the edge without crossing content. The common palette is cream paper, dark teal ink, mustard selection, coral exception, and green completion, with symbols and words supporting every color state. Original shelf tags, customer orders, route cards, prompts, staff cards, pause controls, and shift reports reuse these shapes. Paper grain, stamps, and playful stickers earn their space; decoration cannot lower contrast or occupy an unused page.

## Three font pairings separate personality from reading

Verified primary distributions provide SIL Open Font License 1.1 notices for all five requested candidates and Atkinson Hyperlegible. OFL permits commercial bundling and embedding subject to its conditions, including preserving notices and respecting declared Reserved Font Names for modified fonts. ([Official license](https://openfontlicense.org/open-font-license-official-text/)) The fetched files and license provenance are recorded in the [download manifest](</D:/Business as Usual/research_notes/Phase 8A visual rescue/font-download-manifest.json>). Six comparison TTFs are static, parse successfully, and cover printable ASCII; that does not prove runtime TMP rendering quality.

| Candidate | Verified character/distribution | Proposed use and concern |
|---|---|---|
| Bricolage Grotesque | Expressive grotesque; static cuts available; variable family includes weight, width, optical size | Recommended headings; smaller optical cut for labels; avoid heavy display cut on receipts ([Foundry](https://github.com/ateliertriay/bricolage)) |
| Dela Gothic One | Heavy, flat poster/packaging face; one Regular style | Short display headings; broad forms can crowd long Latin titles ([Description/license](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/delagothicone)) |
| Fredoka | Rounded headline family; static weight choices available | Softer town headings; extensive use risks an overly childish tone ([Distribution/license](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/fredoka)) |
| M PLUS Rounded 1c | Seven static styles; proportional Latin and Japanese coverage | Reader alternative for future Japanese support; small Latin rendering still needs testing ([Distribution/license](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/roundedmplus1c)) |
| Yusei Magic | Permanent-marker character; one Regular style | Occasional route notes; thin strokes and one weight constrain small-size hierarchy ([Distribution/license](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/yuseimagic)) |
| Atkinson Hyperlegible | Distinguishes forms including B/8, O/0, and 1/I/i/l; original static Regular/Bold available | Common reader for IDs, prices, counts; design intent is not a universal accessibility guarantee ([Braille Institute](https://www.brailleinstitute.org/freefont/), [Distribution/license](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/atkinsonhyperlegible)) |

Compare **A: Bricolage ExtraBold + Atkinson Regular/Bold**, **B: Dela Regular + Atkinson**, and **C: Fredoka Bold + Atkinson** using precisely the same ticket, illustrations, viewport, colors, and dimensions. The common strings include **Battery replacement**, **Open Hood / Check Battery / Grab Replacement / Replace & Test / Close & Sign**, **E Open Hood**, **Bay 1 / Van 08 · 2/5**, **$19.90 · 07:48**, and **O0 1Il B8 5689**. A is the recommended direction; B offers stronger packaging character but less space efficiency; C offers warmth but weaker workplace credibility. These judgments remain recommendations until actual comparison captures are inspected.

Proposed type scale at a 1280×720 reference uses Bricolage 40 for large menu display, 32 for handbook title, 24 for section heading; Atkinson 18 for body/receipt, 20 Bold for prompts/counts, and 14–16 for nonessential status. Body line height begins around 1.35×, with an 8 px spacing grid and 24 px panel inset. Use real weights, not synthetic bold, oversized outlines, or separate X/Y scaling. All six fetched fonts have proportional default numerals; hold timers in fixed cells and right-align amount columns until actual numeral substitution support is verified.

TMP's Font Asset Creator converts a source font into an atlas and material with a chosen character set. ([Unity documentation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@4.0/manual/FontAssetsCreator.html)) Proposed implementation starts from actual static TTFs and an SDF atlas containing the UI's real characters, then inspects sampling, punctuation, currency, kerning, fallbacks, and weight mappings in the player. Preserve source files and exact notices. A 1024² atlas is an experiment, not a proven requirement. Captures must cover 1280×720, 1920×1080, 1366×768, 1920×1200, and resizing. No font is production-ready merely because its binary imported.

## Tactile feedback must reveal state without inventing success

**Proposed physical contract:** every prop answers what it is, where it belongs, and what changed. Vehicle wheels touch the floor; a hood follows its hinge; a battery sits in a supported compartment; cartons rest on counters; bags fit inside the cart. Inspect meshes from normal standing and carrying cameras, including approach and post-placement. Do not let initial interpolation visibly move objects from arbitrary origins. Meshy generation supplies art to inspect, not permission to accept malformed geometry. Blender cleanup must establish articulation, scale, sockets, collision, and believable grip points before the asset defines the tutorial illustrations.

Proposed feedback ties input to a reach or button depression immediately, but confirms inventory transfer, tool result, checklist tick, and stamp only when authority accepts the action. Unity's anticipation documentation distinguishes visual response from authoritative state; it supports this principle rather than prescribing BAU's installed package APIs. ([Unity anticipation](https://docs-multiplayer.unity3d.com/netcode/2.1.1/advanced-topics/client-anticipation/)) A rejection returns the local pose and says **Open hood first** or **Hands full**, with a corrective target. Page, tab, receipt, and stamp responses can begin within the user's 0.12–0.3-second design range while input remains responsive. Real hood travel and tool contact need their own believable timing. Reduced motion removes decorative bounce and camera flourish while preserving essential object changes.

Proposed shop work uses a carton flap, visible stock, matching shelf vacancy, product/quantity order card, and physical receipt. Sanitation uses recognizable waste, cart slots, labeled chutes, chosen route order, and visible sorting. Mechanic choices include job priority, inspection thoroughness, and delegated supply; meaningful alternatives follow an understandable basic repair. The shared illustration grammar must survive these differences. None of the professions should become a chain of unexplained extra presses.

**Proposed validation gate:** record a complete solo repair from launch through service report without external instructions, then a solo shop and sanitation shift. Inspect every target, grip, support, animation, objective update, and rejected interaction. Compare the same screens at every requested resolution. Two-player contested pickups must conserve one item and explain both outcomes; four-client testing is a technical check, not the player's launch experience. Private records require restricted delivery, not merely hidden tabs; public clipboard motion must not expose a reader's page. A human novice session must be reported separately from automated checks. Only after those views and flows pass should the implementation be described as a coherent playable rescue.

The decisive change is to make work itself the teaching surface. When objects, illustrations, prompts, and feedback refer to the same action, the handbook can become a useful pocket reference rather than mandatory documentation. Partial receipts and service records can then support future uncertainty because ordinary actions are understandable, while their motives remain open to interpretation.
