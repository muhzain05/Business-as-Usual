# Business as Usual — the cozy social layer after Phase 8B

These are future phases, not implemented Phase 8B features. Ordinary jobs and incomplete evidence remain the center of the deduction game. Leisure should create relationships and plausible errands, not replace work or sell better information.

| Phase | Addition | Social-deduction connection | Smallest useful test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8C | Repeated suspicious days and richer evidence | Several lawful routes and personal motives keep one observed action ambiguous | Rotate requests and motives after first-day human tests; keep correction histories and source-linked claims |
| 9A | Player homes | Private visits, favors and downtime supply believable alibis and secrets | Open exactly as many homes as connected players; leave unused cottages decorative; no automatic criminal-only access |
| 9B | Cozy personalized interiors | Shared music, furniture and invitations let players build trust outside assignments | One small editable room per active home; light decoration, music and personal flavor; readable shared-object rules |
| 9C | Leisure and social spaces | Players choose company, witness each other's errands and develop alliances | Start with one card/poker table or darts corner; later arcades/tabletop distractions; avoid compulsory detached minigames |
| 10A | Earned town money | Useful shifts support a satisfying life in the neighborhood | Earn money from completed work and spend it on cosmetics, decor, home items and optional leisure access |
| 10B | Longer deduction arc | Relationships and repeated responsibilities provide context for conflicting accounts | Test the original four-player/three-day MVP before the six-player/five-day vision; preserve participation and defer stronger meeting powers |

Economy restrictions: no paid or earned deduction advantages, private-information purchases, guilt detectors, better criminal detection, or resource monopolies that hard-lock someone else's job. Cosmetics can be recognizable without becoming certified role tells. Card-game stakes should stay optional and bounded so leisure cannot erase a workday's earnings or dominate the town meter.

Homes must fit the compact geography and actual connected roster. Empty doors are not fake objectives. Cozy interiors should support entertaining visits, not generic rooms with no interactions. Accessibility, privacy, moderation, saved identity and synchronization need explicit designs before persistent housing or an economy ships.

Human gates remain empirical: first-time players can explain the day without coaching; multiple legitimate explanations emerge from the same evidence; all workers enjoy time between investigations; people want another shift. A passing network test does not answer these questions.
