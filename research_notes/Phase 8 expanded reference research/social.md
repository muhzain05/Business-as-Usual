# Phase 8 social deception and social dynamics references

Research date: 2026-10-12. Verified = mechanically supported by linked primary developer/publisher rules or descriptions. Interpretation = design reasoning, not measured evidence of enjoyment. Proposal = a new Business as Usual design, not a reference-game feature. Current store descriptions may reflect updates rather than launch versions. No player studies or comparative retention evidence were located; do not describe inferred fun as proven causation.

## How do ordinary actions become suspicious without proving guilt?

### Takeaway

Ambiguity becomes useful when players can explain the same observed action through several concrete incentives and then spend time, access, or cooperation to test those explanations. A lie should be challengeable through additional evidence, while honest activity must remain compatible with at least some suspicious traces.

### Cited Findings

**Dale & Dawson Stationery Supplies — verified.** Specialists complete workplace objectives; Slackers pursue unproductive activities while impersonating productive workers; the Manager observes and decides whom to fire. Specialists can take coffee breaks. Proximity voice supports accusations and alliances. [Developer Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2920570/Dale__Dawson_Stationery_Supplies/). A documented Specialist camera-firmware task disables surveillance for 30 seconds, and Slackers can exploit the same task. This is a particularly direct verified example of legitimate work producing suspicious circumstances. [Official developer announcements, September 27, 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2920570/announcements/).

**LOCKDOWN Protocol — verified.** The developer describes first-person real-time action and communication, with players cooperating on tasks before time expires and a hidden minority of dissidents trying to prevent completion without being caught. Current description supports up to 16 players; do not repeat the older 3–8-player description as current. [Developer Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/).

**Project Winter — verified.** Survivors gather resources, repair escape objectives, explore buildings, and resist cold and wildlife. Traitors know their teammates, have private radios, and exploit traitor crates and hatches. Proximity voice, radios, text and emotes coexist. Classic and streamlined Cabin Fever modes differ; the current description explicitly mentions blizzards forcing regrouping. [Developer Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/774861/Project_Winter/).

**Blood on the Clocktower — verified.** Players hold secret characters, acquire private nighttime information, and can talk publicly or privately. Some good players have reasons to lie. Death leaves players involved in discussion and team victory, with reduced voting power. [Official rules explanation](https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation). Drunkenness/poisoning can make information or abilities unreliable without the recipient knowing; rules explanations themselves must remain correct. [Official states rules](https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/States).

**The Resistance / Avalon — verified.** Publisher describes an informed evil minority and Merlin, who knows evil but must conceal his own identity. Publisher lists 5–10 players. [Publisher page](https://indieboardsandcards.com/our-games/the-resistance-avalon/). The authored rulebook has public team approval votes and shuffled anonymous quest results; good must submit success, evil may submit success or failure. Rejection does not itself establish evil allegiance. Five consecutive team rejections lose the game for good. [Publisher-authored rulebook, mirrored PDF, pages 4–5, visually inspected](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/a6/dc/c1-the-resistance-avalon-rulebook.pdf). The base Resistance publisher emphasizes participation without player elimination. [Publisher page](https://indieboardsandcards.com/our-games/the-resistance/).

### Inferences

**Dale & Dawson: nine-question comparison.** Enjoyable decision: work efficiently versus maintain a convincing performance of work. Knowledge: one's own assignment and directly observed office activity. Hidden/incomplete: why another employee is taking a break or disabling a camera; seeing a person at a desk does not establish productivity. Alibi: a plausible work assignment, coworker witness, or just-completed errand; challenge by asking what work and why this route. Cooperation/betrayal: real productivity creates joint success while managerial attention makes blame valuable. Surprise: a legitimate break looks like slacking; a necessary camera update hides misconduct. Replayability: different assignments, witness encounters and manager judgments can change social stories; do not claim that they guarantee freshness. Environment: enclosed rooms and workstations make observation local and authority spatial. **BAU proposal:** every risky interaction needs a useful lawful purpose: transfer a parcel, correct a service record, fetch parts, move goods out of rain. Reject Manager firing as BAU's central resolution. Unlike Slackers, BAU's criminal must do enough genuinely useful work to maintain cover.

**LOCKDOWN Protocol: nine-question comparison.** Decision: continue productive work or interrupt it to observe/coordinate. Knowledge: own alignment, task state and nearby events. Hidden/incomplete: who caused delayed progress and whether delay was incompetence or intent. Alibi: a visible contribution or asserted errand, challenged by another player's local observations. Cooperation/betrayal: a shared deadline makes assistance necessary and gives covert obstruction leverage. Surprise: several players trying to solve a practical bottleneck can unintentionally interfere. Replayability: real-time choices and player movement can recombine; verified sources here do not establish exact task randomization. Environment: movement and local interaction determine who sees which action. **BAU proposal:** one shared delivery deadline, persistent carried objects and work bottlenecks that remain fun while deduction is quiet. Take the real-time work pressure, not a combat-first resolution.

**Project Winter: nine-question comparison.** Decision: travel together for safety versus split to gather/repair faster; invest in trust versus risk a stranger's assistance. Knowledge: a survivor sees own resources/actions; a traitor also knows conspirators and has private communication. Hidden/incomplete: other expeditions, resource provenance, why someone returns late. Alibi: claim to have gathered supplies or explored a building; challenge by corroborating stock, route, and companion reports. Cooperation/betrayal: weather and repair dependencies make assistance valuable, creating meaningful later betrayal. Surprise: blizzard regrouping collides with secret plans; an actual environmental problem resembles deliberate delay. Replayability: changing companions, timing and environmental pressures produce different causal sequences; source does not prove particular procedural map generation. Environment: scarce supplies, distributed buildings and exposure force movement and dependence. **BAU proposal:** a stalled van, urgent repair and warehouse delivery create dependencies; shelter/work regrouping substitutes for survival combat. Every delayed delivery needs plausible causes, not a sabotage notification.

**Blood on the Clocktower: nine-question comparison.** Decision: reveal valuable information now, conceal it for protection, or privately test another account. Knowledge: private character/ability results with declared rules about uncertainty. Hidden/unreliable: roles, motivations and information reliability; truthful speakers can report false results. Alibi: consistency among claimed abilities and reported results replaces a spatial alibi. Challenge: compare multiple testimonies and alternate explanations. Cooperation/betrayal: pooling observations helps good, while revealing a source can expose it; evil exploits conflicts. Surprise: several sincere claims can conflict without anyone lying. Replayability: role combinations and socially negotiated interpretations change puzzles. Environment: social topology and private conversations matter more than a simulated town. **BAU proposal:** truthful evidence channels with limited scope, innocent secrets and explicit correction histories. Keep the principle that conflict is not guilt. Do not silently randomize receipt truth or copy invisible poisoning: a blurred serial or late filing needs a discoverable physical cause. BAU should remain a causal world, not an omniscient host deciding which evidence lies.

**Resistance / Avalon: nine-question comparison.** Decision: trust a proposed team, request a different partner, or allow a small setback to protect future access; Merlin chooses how much useful knowledge to reveal. Knowledge: own loyalty; evil knows allies; Merlin knows enemies in the publisher's baseline description. Hidden/incomplete: authorship of individual quest cards and motivation for each public vote. Alibi: successful participation supports an account but evil can choose success; challenge across team composition and repeated outcomes. Cooperation/betrayal: useful early cooperation can buy access to a later decisive objective. Surprise: two collaborators can accidentally overplay a sabotage; successful work does not prove innocence. Replayability: different proposed teams, votes and concealed choices recombine; fixed-table logic may become habitual. Environment: constrained team size and public decision history function as the environment. **BAU proposal:** publicly remembered package custodians and optional coworker requests; shared outcome narrows possibilities without tagging a culprit. Do not copy formal mission teams, Merlin or fail-card sabotage. At four players, a two-person perfectly isolated failure can overidentify guilt; require a legal alternate cause or an earlier third-party access window.

### Gaps

- The LOCKDOWN store page establishes the core loop but not particular physics objects, exact task interactions, postmortem role disclosure, or voting rules. Treat physical comedy examples as BAU proposals unless another researcher verifies them.
- Project Winter has materially changed modes. This analysis uses current official descriptions; do not conflate launch Classic mechanics and Cabin Fever role behavior.
- Dale's announcements mention a broader evolving role pool; the comparison intentionally limits verified role claims to the developer's Manager/Specialist/Slacker explanation.
- No objective study proves the proposed ambiguities are fun for four players. Test them.

## What social pressure can BAU borrow without copying elimination or role systems?

### Takeaway

Public commitments create useful pressure when they change future access or cooperation and can later be compared against outcomes. BAU should make accusations operational—request a joint delivery, ask for a receipt, choose whom to entrust with a parcel—while leaving all four players able to work and speak throughout the day.

### Cited Findings

**Secret Hitler — verified.** Players have hidden identities and publicly approve proposed governments. The President privately draws three policies and discards one; the Chancellor privately discards another and publishes the result. The hidden discards are never shown, so participants may lie about the choices available. Three rejected governments trigger an automatic policy. Private loyalty investigations reveal party membership rather than the special Hitler role; the investigator may lie about the result. Execution removes a player from speech/voting. [Official rules](https://www.secrethitler.com/assets/Secret_Hitler_Rules.pdf).

**The Traitors — verified, limited source access.** Peacock's indexed official explanation establishes shared missions in a castle, concealed traitors, optional recruitment after a traitor's removal, and secretly obtained shields preventing murder. Its full page redirected to a region block during this research. [Official Peacock explanation](https://www.peacocktv.com/blog/the-traitors-game-rules). BBC's original show-format page also could not be fetched; an indexed mirror describes mission cooperation, night murders, and banishment. Use the original BBC link in the report with a source-access caveat, rather than presenting the mirror as a primary publisher. [Original BBC format explanation](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/2xRtJpWRbKcwPdXs7bZnxxH/the-traitors-how-the-show-works).

**Town of Salem 2 — verified.** The current publisher description lists 7–15 players, over 50 roles with distinct abilities/objectives, in-game persuasive chat, alliances, role concealment, and different modes. [Publisher/developer Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2140510/Town_of_Salem_2/). Avoid outdated numerical inventories and exact investigative-result claims: those were not verified against current official role rules in this focused research.

### Inferences

**Secret Hitler: nine-question comparison.** Decision: whom to entrust with authority, whether to accept a bad outcome to preserve momentum, and how strongly to accuse a partner when alternatives were hidden. Knowledge: each participant knows different portions of the policy pipeline; other players see votes/outcomes. Hidden/incomplete: whether the bad policy was forced by the draw or deliberately selected, and whether an investigator reports honestly. Alibi: 'those were my only options'; challenge through another participant's account and later behavior. Cooperation/betrayal: a trusted appointment grants dangerous future authority, making real early cooperation strategically valuable. Surprise: good players can implement the harmful policy honestly; two stories can both be plausible. Replayability: concealed draws, rotating authority and social choices change claims. Environment: the policy pipeline and public tracks shape pressure, not physical geography. **BAU proposal:** a supplier fills a partial order, a courier transfers it, and a mechanic receives it. Everyone sees only their own stage; missing goods may originate upstream or downstream. This produces a grounded contested chain of custody. Reject party scanners, execution, policy tracks and the historical theme. Do not make repeated false accusations costless: a requested recheck consumes work time, not another player's agency.

**The Traitors: nine-question comparison.** Decision: whom to confide in, whose accusation to echo, whether to reveal private protection, and how to preserve a useful alliance despite suspicion. Knowledge: personal allegiance and observed conversations; viewers' privileged edited knowledge must not be mistaken for contestant evidence. Hidden/incomplete: intentions, private alliances and protection possession. Alibi: shared effort and relationship history provide social credibility rather than physical proof. Challenge: ask for consistent accounts and compare commitments; confident body-language interpretation is not reliable guilt evidence. Cooperation/betrayal: shared prize-building rewards productive teamwork even across opposing interests. Surprise: a trusted contributor can have a conflicting end goal; a privately protected person changes expectations. Replayability: cast relationships and missions change social narratives, though this is a produced TV format, not evidence of reusable videogame balance. Environment: shared rooms, private conversations and ceremonies create information bottlenecks and public pressure. **BAU proposal:** a lunchtime reconciliation of deliveries, where each player can volunteer a receipt or ask for a witnessed handoff; no compulsory confession and no elimination. Criminal and innocents should both benefit from healthy town trade. Reject murders, recruitment and daily banishment, and do not use secret shields as magical innocence.

**Town of Salem 2: nine-question comparison.** Decision: which account to disclose, whom to trust with an observation, and whether a claim fits the possibilities of the match. Knowledge: one's abilities/objective and their private results; public chat adds a contested record. Hidden/incomplete: identities and conflicting goals, with role-dependent evidence. Alibi: claimed identity plus action history; challenge through contradictions in reports. Cooperation/betrayal: different objectives make identical public behavior serve different purposes. Surprise: multiple hidden abilities/objectives collide and create apparently inconsistent testimony. Replayability: roles and modes produce different configurations, but the cognitive burden is high compared with a four-person work sim. Environment: the retrieved source establishes thematic Salem presentation, not a freely traversed evidence sandbox. **BAU proposal:** distinct professional lenses—shop inventory, courier transfer records, mechanic service knowledge—without exhaustive role claims or ability result strings. An innocent private favor should explain a withheld receipt; guilt follows from causal inconsistency, not 'you did not claim your role'. Reject a sprawling role catalog, night-kill centrality, direct allegiance results, and forcing every player into public claim templates.

**Cross-reference conclusion / proposed social alternatives.** Use witnessed handoffs, voluntary route disclosure, shared inspections and partner choice as public commitments. Let refusal create an explanation request rather than automatic conviction. Give investigators a real tradeoff: checking a suspect warehouse route delays a job they also need completed. Give the criminal a useful reason to cooperate: their cover and access require a solvent, operating town. Give an innocent a reason to conceal a minor side favor without rewarding sabotage of the entire cooperative objective. Do not let one accusation permanently revoke a player's working life; suspected players must retain ordinary lawful routes and the opportunity to produce better evidence.

### Gaps

- The Traitors varies by national edition and season; this research does not establish universally applicable shield, recruitment, endgame reveal or traitor-count rules. Keep the analysis format-level, explicitly distinguish inference, and do not rely on a particular season twist.
- Town of Salem 2's current store now includes Mafia Pack DLC; do not assert that Coven is universally its only organized evil faction. Exact role outputs need separate verification before using them as technical precedent.
- Secret Hitler and Avalon target at least five people; Town of Salem 2 targets at least seven; Project Winter targets eight. Their social ambiguity cannot be assumed to survive direct compression to four players.

## What should the four-player prototype test?

### Takeaway

Test one transferable object through three professions, two independent evidence sources and one meaningful legal source of suspicious activity. The causal mystery must survive ordinary conversation without requiring a perfect hidden-role algebra puzzle or withholding all information.

### Cited Findings

- Dale's lawful camera downtime supplies a verified precedent for ambiguous work; Avalon's success-by-evil rule supplies a verified precedent for true cooperation failing to certify loyalty. [Dale announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2920570/announcements/); [Avalon authored rules, page 5](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/a6/dc/c1-the-resistance-avalon-rulebook.pdf).
- Clocktower retains dead players' participation; Resistance advertises no elimination. These support examining participation-preserving social consequences, although neither specifies BAU's design. [Clocktower rules](https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation); [Resistance publisher](https://indieboardsandcards.com/our-games/the-resistance/).

### Inferences

**Proposed test slice.** Four players, one covert-objective holder among them, three professions with one profession shared. One day, roughly 15–20 minutes as an initial test duration. Deliver a numbered parts crate through shop dispatch, courier custody and mechanic intake; the covert objective is to divert one component while retaining legitimate work credibility. One innocent is quietly correcting an earlier paperwork mistake; another does a private lawful favor. Each secret causes a observable trace, and none changes allegiance. Professional data can be shared but is not globally broadcast.

**Causal evidence rules.** A receipt proves what was recorded at dispatch, not that contents were later unchanged. A van log proves a vehicle visit, not who drove or what was carried. A witness sees someone holding a crate, not its contents or intent. A corrected service record keeps the old text and author/time of correction, without any guilt classification. The criminal manipulates physical custody or records through the same interactions used for legitimate jobs. Observer tools during testing record complete truth privately for diagnosis; player-facing records remain scoped.

**Design opportunities informed by this research.** (1) Useful legal actions and covert actions share verbs; (2) real early criminal cooperation builds cover; (3) material handoffs create alibis and disputes; (4) private benign favors diversify motives; (5) optional witnessed work tests trust without ending anyone's round; (6) work deadlines price investigation; (7) route separation creates incomplete witnesses; (8) a short public reconciliation makes commitments memorable; (9) correctable records make truthful contradictions possible; (10) shared town value makes criminal productivity genuinely useful. These are proposed designs, not verified mechanics shared by all references.

**Initial acceptance criteria, proposed rather than empirically validated.** Run at least six rounds, rotate professions and culprit, and compare baseline against the custody/evidence/secret variant. Ask every player for their explanation before revealing full truth. Record: distinct lawful explanations entertained; useful corroboration questions; deductions relying on at least two sources; whether innocence could be explained causally; productive cooperation by culprit; idle time; time spent blocked from work; whether suspicion felt arbitrary; and a 1–5 fun rating during work-only periods. Retain a mechanic only if stories become more specific and players remain occupied. If one source solves the culprit immediately, reduce that source's scope rather than making it randomly false. If every innocent tells the whole truth immediately with no tradeoff, adjust minor private incentives. If nobody can reason, improve readable physical causality and access to independent evidence. At four players, avoid evidence narrowing to a uniquely identified two-person sabotage group unless innocent mishap is concretely possible.

### Gaps

- Effort estimates require the parent agent's codebase analysis. These notes deliberately do not claim any of the proposed systems already exists.
- The test duration, round count and acceptance measures are first-pass experimental choices, not benchmark-derived balance requirements.
